# How to Drape and Style a Kasavu Saree for Any Occasion

A Kasavu saree looks effortless when it's worn well, but that effortlessness is the result of a specific drape, the right blouse choice, and a few styling decisions that most people only learn through trial and error. Whether you're wearing one for the first time or you just want to refine your look, here's a complete, practical guide.

## Understanding the Two Main Drape Styles

Before you drape, it helps to know there are actually two distinct traditions at play here:

**Mundum Neriyathum** is the original two-piece Kerala drape — a mundu (lower garment) paired with a neriyathu (the upper piece worn over the blouse and draped like a stole or pallu). This is the older, more traditional form and is what you'll see at classical Kerala weddings and cultural functions.

**Saree-style Kasavu** is the modern, single six-to-nine-yard drape that most people wear today. It follows a standard saree drape (typically the Nivi style) but uses the Kasavu fabric with its gold border, so it retains the traditional look while being easier and quicker to wear.

Most Eliswa customers today wear the saree-style drape for its convenience, while the traditional set-saree drape is often reserved for weddings or classical dance performances.

## Step-by-Step: The Standard Nivi-Style Kasavu Drape

1. **Tuck and pleat the bottom half.** Tuck one end of the saree into your petticoat at the navel and wrap it around your waist once, keeping the bottom edge even with the floor.
2. **Make the front pleats.** Create 5–7 even pleats about 5 inches wide, hold them together, and tuck them into the petticoat just left of center, so they fall in a neat vertical line down the front.
3. **Wrap the remaining fabric around.** Take the rest of the saree around your back and bring it over your left shoulder.
4. **Pleat the pallu.** For Kasavu sarees, keep the pallu pleats slightly looser and wider than usual since the gold border is the star. Let the border show clearly along the edge rather than folding it under.
5. **Pin at the shoulder.** A single pin at the shoulder keeps the pallu in place through a long day of festivities without restricting movement.

## Getting the Border to Show Properly

The entire appeal of a Kasavu saree lies in that gold border, so the biggest styling mistake is draping it in a way that hides it. When you make your front pleats, angle them so a sliver of the golden border peeks through at the hem. On the pallu, avoid overly tight, narrow pleats, since they bunch the border and make it look cramped, a slightly looser pallu pleat lets the border drape in a visible unbroken line.

## Choosing the Right Blouse

Because the saree body is plain, the blouse is where you can bring in personality:

- **Traditional route**: a simple gold-bordered or solid-cream blouse keeps the entire look classic and minimal, ideal for temple visits or older-generation family functions.
- **Contemporary route**: a contrast-colour blouse in maroon, emerald green, or mustard adds visual interest and works especially well for photos.
- **Statement route**: an embroidered or embellished blouse with the same gold zari as the saree border ties the whole outfit together for weddings and bigger events.

Sleeve length matters too. Elbow-length or three-quarter sleeves are the most versatile, working for both daytime and evening functions, while sleeveless blouses tend to suit warmer, more casual settings.

## Jewelry That Works With Kasavu

Because a Kasavu saree is visually simple, jewelry does a lot of the heavy lifting:

- **Temple jewelry** — gold jewelry with temple motifs (peacocks, deities, floral patterns) is the classic pairing and complements the traditional aesthetic perfectly.
- **Jimikki earrings** — the traditional bell-shaped Kerala earrings are almost a signature pairing with Kasavu.
- **Layered necklaces** — a short choker paired with a slightly longer chain adds dimension without overwhelming the plain saree body.
- **Bangles over rings** — a stack of gold bangles reads more traditional and photographs better than heavy rings.

## Hair and Makeup Notes

Fresh jasmine flowers (mulla poo) pinned into a bun are the classic finishing touch for any Kasavu look, especially during Onam. For makeup, a warm, dewy base with a bold kumkum-red or maroon lip tends to complement the gold-and-cream palette better than cooler tones. Keep eye makeup relatively soft, since the overall look benefits from letting the saree and jewelry stand out rather than competing with a heavy smoky eye.

## Styling for Different Occasions

**Office or daytime festive wear**: Pure cotton Kasavu, simple gold jewelry, minimal makeup, hair left open or in a low bun.

**Temple visits or religious functions**: Traditional mundum neriyathum drape or a plain cotton Kasavu saree, temple jewelry, and jasmine flowers.

**Weddings and receptions**: Tissue or silk-cotton Kasavu, an embellished blouse, layered jewelry, and a more elaborate hairstyle.

**Family photographs (like Onam family portraits)**: Saree-style Kasavu with the border draped prominently, coordinated blouse colours across family members, and simple, photograph-friendly jewelry like jimikki earrings and bangles.

## Common Draping Mistakes to Avoid

- Pleating the pallu too tightly, which hides the border.
- Wearing the wrong petticoat colour underneath, a petticoat that's visibly different from the saree's off-white shade can peek through and look mismatched.
- Skipping the pin at the shoulder, which leads to the pallu slipping throughout the day.
- Over-accessorizing with too many contrasting colours, when the saree itself is meant to be the quiet, elegant centerpiece.

## Final Thoughts

A Kasavu saree rewards a little bit of intention. Getting the drape right so the border shows clearly, choosing a blouse that complements rather than competes, and picking jewelry that leans into the traditional gold-and-cream palette are all it takes to go from "wearing a saree" to genuinely owning the look. Once you've draped one a few times, it becomes second nature, and it's one of those outfits that consistently gets compliments no matter how many times you wear it.
