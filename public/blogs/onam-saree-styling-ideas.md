# What to Wear for Onam: Styling Your Kasavu Saree for Every Onam Event

Onam isn't a single day, it's a ten-day celebration, and most families move through several distinct moments: setting up the Athapookkalam, the big Thiruvonam feast, evening family gatherings, Pulikali processions, and temple visits. Wearing the same styling for all of them rarely works. Here's how to adapt your Kasavu saree look across the full festival.

## Morning: Athapookkalam Styling

The flower carpet, or pookalam, is usually arranged early in the morning, often by the women of the household, and involves a fair amount of bending, crouching, and arranging petals. Comfort should come first here.

- **Saree**: a lightweight pure cotton Kasavu, nothing with heavy embellishment that could get in the way or get dirty.
- **Drape**: keep the pallu pinned securely at the shoulder so it doesn't trail into the flowers while you're bent over arranging the design.
- **Jewelry**: minimal, a pair of simple gold studs and bangles are enough, save the heavier pieces for later in the day.
- **Hair**: a low bun or braid keeps hair out of the way entirely.

## Thiruvonam Day: The Main Look

This is the centerpiece of the festival, and the styling should reflect that.

- **Saree**: your finest Kasavu cotton or tissue saree, ideally the one bought specifically as "Onakodi" for the year.
- **Drape**: the classic Nivi-style drape with the pallu pleated so the gold border shows prominently across the shoulder and front.
- **Blouse**: a well-fitted gold-bordered or contrast-colour blouse, this is the one day where a slightly more festive blouse choice, like a subtle embroidered neckline, feels appropriate.
- **Jewelry**: temple jewelry is the classic pairing, think gold necklaces with peacock or floral motifs, along with jimikki earrings.
- **Hair**: fresh jasmine flowers pinned into a bun is close to a non-negotiable finishing touch for Thiruvonam.
- **Makeup**: a warm base with a bold red or maroon lip complements the cream-and-gold palette beautifully.

## Onasadya: Styling for the Feast

Since the sadya involves sitting on the floor or at a low table for an extended meal, practicality matters as much as looks.

- **Saree**: lightweight cotton or a soft silk-cotton blend that won't feel restrictive while seated.
- **Drape**: keep the pleats slightly looser than usual for ease of movement, and avoid an overly tight petticoat.
- **Jewelry**: simpler pieces you won't need to fuss with while eating, smaller earrings and a single necklace rather than layered pieces.
- **Practical tip**: tuck the pallu securely so it doesn't dip near the banana leaf while you're eating.

## Evening Functions and Pulikali Viewing

Evenings during Onam often mean family gatherings, cultural programs, or watching the Pulikali (tiger dance) processions, all settings where a bit more shine works well, especially under evening lighting.

- **Saree**: tissue Kasavu or a silk-cotton blend for extra sheen that photographs beautifully under artificial light.
- **Blouse**: a slightly more elaborate blouse, perhaps with zari work matching the saree border, works well here.
- **Jewelry**: layered necklaces and statement earrings suit the more festive evening mood.
- **Hair**: a half-up style or loose waves offer a nice contrast to the more formal bun worn earlier in the day.

## Temple Visits During the Onam Period

Many families visit the temple at some point during Onam, and the styling for this should lean more conservative and traditional.

- **Saree**: plain cotton Kasavu without heavy embellishment.
- **Jewelry**: minimal and traditional, small gold studs, a simple chain.
- **Overall approach**: modesty and simplicity are the priorities here rather than festive shine.

## Mom-and-Daughter or Family Twinning Looks

A growing trend during Onam, especially for family photographs, is coordinating outfits across generations.

- Pair a mother's Kasavu saree with a daughter's half-saree or pavadai in the same gold-and-cream palette.
- Coordinate blouse colours across family members even if the saree styles differ slightly by age.
- For young children, kasavu-inspired frocks or skirt-and-top sets keep them in the same visual theme without needing a full saree drape.

## Photography Tips for Onam Family Portraits

Since so many Onam moments end up as family photographs, a few styling choices make a real difference in how the pictures turn out:

- The gold border photographs best when it's draped visibly rather than tucked away, so double-check your pallu pleating before photos.
- Warmer, natural light (like late afternoon) brings out the zari's shine far better than harsh midday sun or indoor fluorescent lighting.
- Coordinating, rather than exactly matching, colours across family members tends to look more natural in group photos than everyone wearing identical outfits.

## Final Thoughts

The beauty of dressing for Onam is that the same core garment, the Kasavu saree, can be restyled across an entire ten-day festival simply by adjusting the fabric weight, jewelry, and draping for each moment. A lighter cotton and minimal styling for the practical morning tasks, your finest piece for Thiruvonam itself, and a bit more shine for the evening festivities means you're dressed appropriately, and comfortably, through the whole celebration.
